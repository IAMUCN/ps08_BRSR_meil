Based on the video conversation with Dr. P. Ravi Reddy (Director at MEIL), here is an analysis of the Needs, Deliverables, and Recommended Approach for Problem Statement 08 regarding the ESG/BRSR reporting portal.



1\. The Core Need (Why this is required)

The Scale \& Complexity: MEIL is a massive infrastructure conglomerate executing $9-10 billion worth of work annually. They have 20–28 subsidiaries and handle 250 to 300 live projects across India and abroad \[06:01].



Data Collection Bottleneck: Currently, collecting sustainability data (fuel consumption, water sourcing, power usage, waste management, employee data) manually from 350-400 different business units is a "herculean task" \[06:37].



Regulatory Alignment: While SEBI currently mandates BRSR (Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report) only for the top 1,000 listed companies, MEIL wants to implement this voluntarily \[05:16]. Since the SEBI framework acts as the Indian standard for the UN’s Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), a solution built for MEIL’s complex hierarchy will essentially be industry-ready for any Indian corporate \[12:43].



2\. The Expected Deliverables

The overarching deliverable is a comprehensive web-based ESG Reporting Software, which specifically must include:



Multi-Level Data Entry Portal: Interfaces that allow personnel from across 250+ project sites to log in and enter monthly data, attach documentary evidence, and apply necessary conversion factors \[11:25].



Carbon Equivalent Conversions: The portal needs built-in logic to convert raw usage into standard Carbon Emissions metrics. For example, if a project uses a specific type of coal (like lignite) or buys green power vs. grid power, the software must calculate the correct carbon equivalent using international scientific tables \[09:38].



Scope 1 and Scope 2 Focus: The immediate deliverables should heavily focus on Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions (direct fuels, power, and water). Incorporating Scope 3 emissions (like employee travel carbon footprints) is a bonus \[11:54].



SEBI BRSR Compliance: The software's output must map perfectly to the 50-60 specific data submission formats dictated by the SEBI BRSR PDF guidelines \[05:41].



3\. Recommended Approach for Participants

Dr. Reddy provided explicit advice to students on how to tackle this hackathon problem:



Form Multidisciplinary Teams: This is not just a software engineering problem; it’s a data-heavy business problem. It involves HR data, environmental metrics, and business processes. He highly recommends that B.Tech students team up with MBA or HR students to understand the full scope \[13:41].



R\&D on Conversion Metrics: Do not wait to be spoon-fed the data. Participants need to actively research standard international tables to figure out carbon footprint equivalencies (e.g., converting 1 kg of CNG into carbon equivalents) \[09:33].



Functionality \& Completeness > Perfection: Dr. Reddy emphasized avoiding the "quest for perfection that leads to paralysis" \[14:35]. MEIL prefers a comprehensive software that attempts to cover the entire BRSR scope—even if it has minor errors—over a beautifully polished app that only covers a small fraction of the requirements \[15:30].



Key Takeaway for the Hackathon: Don't get stuck perfecting a single module. Read the SEBI PDF thoroughly, map out the data structures, and build a portal that functions end-to-end for a massive, multi-tiered organization.
